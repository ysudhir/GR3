Agentic Reg Ops

A design for translating global payments data into regulatory reports across every operating jurisdiction — by capturing each country's requirements, in any language, as one portable format in an llm-wiki that AI agents draft, Reg Ops humans approve, and a Regulatory Knowledge Base serves for regulatory reports, RFIs, audits, and ad hoc extracts.

§1Problem & thesis

A global payments franchise files regulatory returns in every market it operates in — transaction-level reports, balance-of-payments statistics, central-bank returns, AML filings. Each obligation arrives as a circular, gazette notice, portal announcement, or schema release, written in the regulator's language and amended continually. A sample of what the intake actually looks like:

JurisdictionRegulator & returnSource languageCadence

Illustrative rows — the real inventory spans 60+ markets and hundreds of distinct returns.

The data side of this problem is largely solved: payments already flow through canonical stores. The unsolved side is the interpretation layer — what each regulator actually requires, which transactions are in scope, what the thresholds and deadlines are, how local terms map to our data elements. Today that layer lives in country spreadsheets, email threads, vendor PDFs, and the heads of long-tenured Reg Ops staff. Every RFI, audit, and new report build re-derives it from scratch, and no two derivations agree.

The thesis of this design: make the interpretation layer itself a governed, versioned corpus. Every obligation becomes one Global Regulatory Requirement Record (GR3) — a single portable format readable by humans and parseable by LLMs — living in an llm-wiki where agents draft and Reg Ops approves. On every approval the wiki compiles into the Regulatory Knowledge Base (RKB), which drives generation of the regulatory reports themselves, answers RFIs, assembles audit evidence packs, and serves ad hoc extracts — always with exact citations back to the original-language clause, so every figure in every filing traces to a clause.

Position of record

The llm-wiki is the system of record for regulatory intent. Payment systems remain the record for transactions; the wiki records what regulators require and how we interpreted it — versioned, approved, and citable.

§2The system at a glance

Five stages, one direction of flow, one human control point. Agents move material from raw sources into draft records; nothing passes the wiki boundary without a country SME's approval; everything downstream of the wiki is deterministic compilation.

Reading the diagram left to right: capture is continuous and automated; drafting is agent work product, always in the shape of a wiki pull request; the wiki is where interpretation is fixed and approved; the RKB is a deterministic build artifact of the wiki; consumers never read raw sources or agent output directly — only compiled, approved records.

§3The portable format: the GR3

The Global Regulatory Requirement Record is the unit of the whole system — one record per obligation-and-return pair, per jurisdiction. It is a Markdown file with YAML frontmatter, and that choice is deliberate: humans read and diff it in review, LLMs parse it without a bespoke toolchain, CI lints it mechanically, and git versions it for free. Alternatives were rejected on those axes — database rows have no narrative and diff poorly; a prose-only wiki cannot be validated or compiled; a rules DSL alone excludes the humans who must sign off.

Two planes, one record

Every record encodes each obligation twice, and the two planes serve different consumers:

  • The fact plane (frontmatter). Machine-readable YAML: identity, legal basis, trigger rules, thresholds with currency, deadlines as calendar rules, the data contract, lifecycle state. This is what the RKB structured store and the report generator consume.
  • The narrative plane (body). Prose sections: interpretation notes, edge cases, exemption history, SME commentary. This is what RFI answers quote and new joiners read.

A CI check (an agent cross-reading both planes) flags drift between the prose description of a trigger and its machine-evaluable twin, so the planes cannot silently diverge.

Illustrative GR3 — Germany, Bundesbank AWV Z4 external payments report

type: gr3.record                                  # OKF-conformant
title: External Payments Report (Z4) — Zahlungen im Außenwirtschaftsverkehr
jurisdiction: DE
regulator: Deutsche Bundesbank
legal_basis:

    source: src/de/awv-2024.de.pdf#sec-67          # original German, clause anchor
    lang: de
report:
  code: Z4
  channel: Bundesbank AMS portal
  format: XML (AMS schema v3)
obligation:
  who: resident entities, including bank branches
  trigger:
    prose: >
      Incoming or outgoing cross-border payments above EUR 12,500 or
      equivalent, excluding goods invoices settled within 12 months.
    rule: |                                         # machine-evaluable twin
      amount_eur > 12500
      and counterparty.country != "DE"
      and "goods_settlement_short_term" not in exemptions
  frequency: monthly
  deadline: { calendar: "7th calendar day of following month", tz: Europe/Berlin }
data_contract:
  - element: dict:payment.amount        # ISO 20022 pacs.008 …/IntrBkSttlmAmt
  - element: dict:counterparty.country  # ISO 20022 pacs.008 …/Cdtr/PstlAdr/Ctry
  - element: dict:purpose.code          # maps to Bundesbank Kennzahl list
lifecycle:
  status: effective
  effective_from: 2025-01-01
  knowledge_from: 2024-11-18            # when we approved this interpretation
  supersedes: gr3:de.bbk.awv-z4@4.1.0
  review_by: 2026-11-01                 # staleness guard — SME must re-affirm
  approvals:
    maker: agent.regops-extractor@2.4
    checker: sme.de-payments
    four_eyes: regops.lead-emea
translations:
  - lang: en
    of: src/de/awv-2024.de.pdf
    method: agent.translate@2.3, human-reviewed, segment-aligned

Bilingual discipline

The original-language text is evidence; the English normalization is interpretation. Both are kept forever. Source documents live in the wiki with stable, clause-level anchors; translations are versioned artifacts with provenance (which agent, which model version, who reviewed), aligned to the original segment by segment so any citation can show both languages side by side. A translation is never allowed to replace or overwrite its source, and every fact in the frontmatter must carry an anchor into an original-language clause — CI rejects records with unanchored claims.

Bitemporal lifecycle

Regulatory questions are questions about time, and there are two clocks. effective_from records when the obligation applies in the world; knowledge_from records when we approved this interpretation. Audits ask both — "what did the rule require in March?" and "what did you believe it required in March?" — so every version carries both dates and the RKB can answer as-of queries on either axis (§7).

§4The llm-wiki

The wiki is a git repository of Markdown pages — not a hosted wiki product. Git is the feature: pull requests are the maker-checker workflow, diffs are the change review, history is the audit trail, and branch protection is the control that nothing merges without the right approvals. A thin web layer renders pages, resolves links, and gives Reg Ops staff a review UI that hides git mechanics; agents interact with the repo directly.

Pages reference each other with typed links — implements, supersedes, maps-to, derived-from — and those links are data, not decoration: the RKB compiles them into the knowledge graph that answers impact questions like "which returns break if dict:purpose.code changes."

CI as the first reviewer

Every pull request runs a lint gate before a human sees it: frontmatter schema validation, every-fact-has-an-anchor, link integrity, effective-date sanity (no overlapping versions of the same GR3), prose/rule consistency (§3), and property tests running the machine-evaluable rule against a fixture set of sample transactions. Agents produce a lot of drafts; CI keeps human review time for judgment, not typo-hunting.

§5JARO AI Agents & the human loop

Six agent roles, each narrow, each producing artifacts a human can check. No agent approves anything; every agent's output is a draft in the wiki's format.

Where the humans are

Reg Ops is not reviewing agent chatter — it is reviewing pull requests in a format built for review. The country SME is the checker on every record in their jurisdiction: they read the diff, the anchored clauses (in both languages), and the CI results, then approve or request changes. A four-eyes approver — Reg Ops lead or compliance, by risk tier — co-signs anything entering effective state. Interpretation notes record contested readings with named deciders, so the next auditor sees not just the conclusion but who reached it and why. The design goal is that human hours shift from transcribing regulation to judging interpretations — the work only humans can do.

§6Governing the agents

A system that lets agents draft regulatory interpretations must be able to show an auditor how those agents were controlled. The fleet therefore runs as governed agents inside the JARO runtime, and the same evidence chain that governs the corpus governs its authors:

Governance controlApplied here
Policy enforcementEvery write action is policy-checked at runtime: agents may draft and comment, never approve or merge; data-residency policies route in-country documents to in-country model endpoints where law requires it
Content guardrailsExtraction and translation outputs pass guardrail profiles — PII screening, and a grounding validator that rejects any drafted fact lacking a source-clause citation
Dynamic promptsPer-jurisdiction extraction and translation prompts are versioned, governed resources — a prompt change is itself reviewable, and every draft records which prompt version produced it
Governance eventsEvery agent action emits a signed governance event; an GR3's audit trail therefore includes not only who approved it but exactly which agent, model, prompt, and policy context drafted it
Data lineageAd hoc extracts (§8) are registered lineage resources, so every extract carries what was queried, from which GR3 versions, by whom, for which request

This closes the loop an auditor will actually probe: not just "is the requirement record correct?" but "how do you know the machine that wrote the first draft was operating inside its mandate?" Both questions get the same answer — a citable event chain.

§7The RKB: compiled, not crawled

The RKB is not a search engine pointed at documents — it is a deterministic build artifact of the wiki. On every merge to main, a compiler reads the changed pages and refreshes three synchronized indexes. Because the input is structured (frontmatter, typed links, clause anchors), the compiler never guesses at chunking: facts land in the structured store, prose sections become citation-bearing passages, links become graph edges.

The retrieval contract

Everything that reads the RKB honors three rules:

  1. Only approved-and-effective is served. Drafts and in-review records are invisible unless a caller explicitly requests preview scope — and preview responses are watermarked as non-authoritative.
  2. Every answer cites. Responses carry {gr3, version, anchor, quote, lang} tuples; an answer the copilot cannot anchor to a clause is an answer it refuses to give.
  3. Time is a parameter. Queries accept as_of_effective and as_of_knowledge, resolved against the version chain — because "what does the rule say?" and "what did it say last March, and when did we learn that?" are different audit questions with different right answers.

The vector index uses multilingual embeddings so retrieval crosses languages: an English RFI about German reporting thresholds surfaces the anchored German clause alongside its aligned translation. Keyword search (report codes, form numbers, legal cites) and graph traversal (impact, coverage, supersession) round out retrieval — three access paths over one corpus.

§8Serving reports, RFIs, audits & extracts

RFI

A regulator asks why Q1 Z4 filings excluded a class of intercompany flows. The RFI copilot retrieves the governing GR3 as of the filing period (both clocks), the anchored AWV clause in German with its aligned translation, the interpretation note where the exclusion was decided and by whom, and the lineage of the filings themselves. It drafts a response with every claim cited; a Reg Ops human edits and sends. Nothing in the draft exists that the RKB cannot anchor.

Audit

An audit evidence pack is a single assembled artifact per question: the GR3 version in force for the period, its source documents and checksums, the full approval chain (maker, checker, four-eyes, timestamps), the governance events for the agents that drafted it (§6), and the filings generated under it. Because the wiki is git and the RKB is bitemporal, "show me exactly what you believed on 14 March and how you came to believe it" is a query, not a three-week document hunt.

Ad hoc extracts

A request like "all payments to sanctioned-adjacent corridors above local reporting thresholds, last quarter" resolves in two steps: the RKB translates the ask into an extract spec — which dict: elements, which per-jurisdiction thresholds from which GR3 versions, which period — and the data platform executes the spec against the canonical payments store. The extract ships with a lineage stamp naming every GR3 version consulted, so the numbers remain defensible after the fact.

Regulatory reports

The reports themselves are the RKB's largest consumer: the report generator pulls each GR3's data contract, trigger rules, and filing calendar from the structured store — the same records, citations, and as-of semantics as every other consumer. §9 details the runtime; the point here is that report production has no privileged side channel. If it is not in an approved GR3, it is not in a filing.

§9From wiki to filings

The same records that answer questions also generate the reports — which is what keeps interpretation and production from drifting apart. The report generator reads an approved GR3's data contract, trigger rules, and calendar; selects transactions from the canonical payments store; renders the regulator's target format; and validates the output against the same rules before submission.

Acknowledgments, rejections, and resubmissions are tracked against the GR3 version that produced the filing, which feeds §10: a spike in rejections on one return is a signal the record — not just the pipeline — may need revisiting.

§10Change management

Amendments are the steady state, so the amendment path is the main path:

  1. Detect. The horizon scanner flags a new circular and diffs it against the source pages already on file.
  2. Assess impact. A graph query answers what the change touches: which GR3s cite the amended clauses, which dictionary elements they use, which filings those drive.
  3. Draft the next version. The extractor produces gr3:…@N+1 with a future effective_from, superseding — never editing — the current version.
  4. Review and approve. Same maker-checker gate as any record; higher risk tiers pull compliance into the four-eyes step.
  5. Parallel-run and switch. Where the change alters report content, both versions generate during a parallel window; the calendar gates the cutover, and the old version remains queryable forever under both clocks.

Two guards keep the corpus from rotting quietly: every GR3 carries a review_by date that pages the country SME for re-affirmation even when no amendment was detected, and the consistency-QA agent treats an expired review date as a finding.

§11Design tenets

  • Originals are evidence; English is interpretation. Source text is immutable and clause-anchored; translations carry provenance and never replace what they translate.
  • Agents draft, humans approve. No agent holds approval authority — enforced by runtime policy, not convention.
  • Fail-closed. Unapproved means invisible: not served by the RKB, not used by the generator, not quoted in an RFI.
  • Every claim cites. A fact without a clause anchor fails CI; an answer without a citation is refused.
  • Two clocks, always. Effective time and knowledge time are both first-class, because audits ask about both.
  • One format for humans and machines. The moment agents and Reg Ops work in different representations, they diverge — the GR3 is the shared surface that prevents it.

§12Rollout

  1. Foundations. Ratify the GR3 schema and the dict: dictionary seeded from ISO 20022; stand up the wiki repo, CI lint gate, and review UI.
  2. Pilot: three jurisdictions. One Latin-script non-English market (Germany), one CJK market (Japan), one English market (Singapore) — chosen to stress translation, extraction, and mapping differently. Agents backfill; SMEs review; measure review time per record and correction rate.
  3. RKB and the RFI copilot. Compile the pilot corpus; put the copilot in front of live RFIs in draft-only mode; assemble the first audit evidence packs from real requests.
  4. Report generation and scale-out. Wire one pilot return end-to-end (GR3 → generator → validator → filing) with a parallel run against the incumbent process, then expand jurisdiction by jurisdiction — the marginal cost of each new market is agent capture plus SME review, not a new system.

The success measure worth tracking from day one: the fraction of RFI and audit responses assembled entirely from RKB citations — because that number is the direct measure of whether the interpretation layer has actually moved out of people's heads and into the record.
